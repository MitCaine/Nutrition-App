# UI-133 — Make Food Details task-first and space nutrient units

## Goal

Make Food Details prioritize the repeated task of inspecting nutrition and logging the currently selected serving without constraining arbitrary Food names to sticky-header width, while making nutrient amount/unit typography easier to scan.

## Outcome

Food Details uses the same single-height route header established elsewhere in the app: Back, generic `Food` title, Log, and conditional Edit share the fixed route row. The full Food name, serving choices, and secondary management controls scroll with content. Changing the selected serving continues to change resolved nutrition and the exact amount passed to Log without duplicating serving text in sticky chrome. User-facing nutrient values use readable number/unit spacing such as `100 kcal`, `1,200 mg`, `10 g`, and `2 mcg`.

## Non-goals

- No Food persistence, API, runtime, SQLite, migration, or source-authority changes.
- No logging-selection or serving-identity redesign.
- No Food-name character limit.
- No swipe-to-reveal or horizontally scrolling Food title.
- No redesign of the shared route-header primitive.
- No change to E4-13 Food-authoring density behavior.
- No E4-14 grouped extended-nutrient authoring work.
- No reclassification or conversion of nutrient units.

## Background

The baseline Food Details route header used the complete Food name as its sticky title, forcing arbitrary user/source data to compete with fixed navigation chrome. UI-133 therefore moved the complete name into ordinary scrollable content and changed the route title to generic `Food`.

The screen already owns the selected resolved amount through `selectedResolvedFoodAmount` and sends that exact selection into logging through `foodDetailLogInitialAmount`. That authority must remain unchanged.

The first UI-133 implementation trial added a second sticky row containing selected amount, Log, and Edit. Native iPhone review showed that this created a one-off enlarged Food header and visually separated Log/Edit from Back even though all three are route-level actions. The accepted correction uses the existing `RouteScreenHeader` trailing action area instead and removes selected amount text from sticky chrome.

Automated regression also showed that generic `formatAmountWithUnit` serves non-nutrient serving/mass presentation. The bounded final typography design therefore preserves that compact generic formatter and uses one shared nutrient-specific spaced formatter for Food, USDA, and aggregate nutrient values.
