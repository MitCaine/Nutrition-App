# GH-132-P4R2 — Increase History crossing-caret contrast

## Goal

Correct the single contrast failure found during GH-132-P4R1 physical
qualification while preserving its accepted caret placement and all P4
crossing semantics.

## Outcome

Every strict above-reference numeric bar retains the exact P4R1 upward-caret
geometry and bar-top anchor.

Each of the two geometric caret segments is rendered twice at identical
coordinates:

- outer layer: `barColor`, 4-pixel rounded stroke;
- inner layer: `selectionColor`, 2-pixel rounded stroke.

The result remains one visual caret with a 1-pixel contrasting halo around the
inner stroke.

This construction is deliberately tied to the existing bar presentation:

- an unselected numeric bar is filled with `barColor`, so the inner
  `selectionColor` stroke remains distinct;
- a selected numeric bar is filled with `selectionColor`, so the outer
  `barColor` stroke remains distinct.

No new semantic color, theme token, hard-coded color, or target/adherence
meaning is introduced.

## Non-goals

- No change to strict-crossing eligibility.
- No change to P4R1 caret coordinates, width, height, or bar-top anchor.
- No change to reference-line position, color, width, or meaning.
- No change to bar fill, selected outline, height, scale, baseline, viewport,
  scrolling, slot width, or touch target.
- No change to exact above-reference text or accessibility wording.
- No target, model, projection, nutrition, theme, backend, API, navigation,
  package, persistence, or History-screen change.

## Background

P4R1 mechanically qualified all six acceptance criteria. Native physical review
confirmed that the caret tip now correctly touches the corresponding column top
for near-reference and materially-above-reference columns.

The remaining physical defect is contrast. The single
`referenceLineColor` caret is too similar in visual value to the blue focused
History bars and is therefore difficult to distinguish at phone scale.

The approved correction preserves geometry and adds local contrast using colors
that the chart already owns.

  halo on each side of the inner stroke.
